# System Architecture

A plain-English guide to how the system is built, what each part does, and how they connect.

---

## The big picture

Think of this system as a trading desk with several specialists who each do one job:

1. **Data Collector** — connects to Interactive Brokers and fetches live prices, option chains, and your account positions.
2. **Analysts** — calculate whether now is a good time to sell options on a stock (IV rank, technical trend, fundamentals, liquidity).
3. **Strategy Team** — generates specific trade candidates: "sell the AAPL July 200 call for $1.50".
4. **Risk Manager** — checks every candidate against hard rules before anything can proceed.
5. **Claude** — reviews the top candidates and writes a plain-English explanation of the tradeoffs.
6. **Telegram** — sends the candidates to your phone and collects your Approve/Reject response.
7. **Executor** — once you approve, re-checks the rules one more time with a live quote, then places the order.
8. **Intraday Watch** — monitors open positions during the day and alerts you when a roll is needed.

**You are the final decision-maker.** No order is ever placed without your explicit approval.

---

## How a trade happens (the pipeline)

```
IBKR live data
    ↓
Analytics (IV rank, technicals, fundamentals, liquidity)
    ↓
Strategy modules (generate candidate trades)
    ↓
Decision Engine (score + rank candidates)
    ↓
Rules Engine ← HARD STOP: enforces all risk limits, no AI involved
    ↓
Claude review (plain-English explanation, optional enrichment)
    ↓
Telegram → YOU approve or reject
    ↓
Rules Engine (re-checks with a fresh live quote) ← SECOND HARD STOP
    ↓
Order placed at IBKR (limit order at mid-price)
    ↓
Fill confirmation sent back to Telegram
```

Every step writes its results to the database. If any step fails, the system can resume from where
it left off. If Claude is unavailable, the pipeline continues without it — Claude enriches but
never blocks.

Per-symbol staleness means a name's clock runs from its own last fetch, so names that move often (and refresh via the move gate anyway) and names that stay quiet naturally desynchronize — periodic refreshes spread out over time on their own, without any explicit batch/rotation schedule. **2026-08-27:** two more thresholds extend the same gate. `market_data.held_position_move_pct` (default `0.02` = 2%, **directional — up only**) replaced what used to be an unconditional "always fetch every held stock position every cycle" rule — previously the single biggest fixed IBKR chain-fetch cost in the loop, since it ignored movement entirely; a held name is now only re-fetched once it has *risen* this much (a new CC candidate needs the extra room a rally creates; a drop doesn't open one, and existing-position risk — delta drift, assignment, rolls — is handled continuously by the separate event-driven monitor, not this gate; a dropping held name is still caught by the per-symbol `force_full_scan_minutes` check above), or has no baseline yet. `market_data.dip_pull_in_pct` (default `0.03` = 3%, **directional — drop only**) governs `would_own` names outside `actively_wheeling` ("dip-watch"): they are not scanned every cycle at all, and are excluded from `force_full_scan_minutes` entirely; they're pulled into a cycle's option-chain scan only once their live spot has *dropped* this much from its last fetch, since a rally is never a CSP entry signal for a name outside the core rotation. Manual `/scan` ignores the whole gate and always sweeps the full universe (dip_watch names seed-only unless gapped ≥3% overnight — see below). **P3-P4 M1 portfolio-spine keys:** `market_data.portfolio_snapshot_interval_minutes` (default 15) — minimum minutes between automatic portfolio snapshots the intraday monitor writes; the gate reads the `portfolio_snapshots` table's newest `captured_at` (not an in-memory clock), so a monitor restart can't burst snapshots, and a `refresh` command ignores it because an operator asking for a fetch gets one; `storage.portfolio_snapshot_retention_days` (default 30) — days of intraday `portfolio_snapshots` history kept, pruned by the EOD run's step 8 beside `purge_old_risk_verdicts` (a year of intraday history needs a rollup, not a longer retention). |

At $100k cash the reserve is `max(100000×0.20, 10000) = 20000` either way, but at a smaller account the absolute floor dominates and is *not* inert — e.g. at $16k cash, `max(16000×0.20, 10000) = 10000` (the 20% alone would have been $3,200). **Job 2, concentration (risk units — `collateral × IV × sqrt(DTE/365)`, so share price is not a risk measure):** `max_risk_units_per_ticker_pct` (5.0) and `max_risk_units_per_sector_pct` (25.0), both a percentage of net liq. **CALIBRATION WARNING (kept verbatim from the config comment):** risk units are collateral scaled by IV × √(DTE/365), typically 0.05–0.30, so these percentages are NOT comparable to the old collateral-based ones — 5.0 here is far more permissive than 5.0 was before. `max_risk_units_per_ticker_pct: 5.0` was examined, not assumed: `scripts/capacity_report.py` at $300k NLV / $100k cash shows AMD's single lot alone costs 3.06% of NLV in risk units (SMH 2.85%, NBIS 1.89%, QQQ 1.78%, META 1.77%) — the floor below which AMD becomes unreachable again — leaving only a 3.1–5.0 viable range; the human ruling kept 5.0, the permissive end. `max_collateral_per_ticker_pct` (10.0) is the fallback cap used when IV is unavailable, applied to raw collateral (strictly more conservative than the risk-unit cap) so a missing IV can never make a position *less* constrained. **Job 3, deliberateness (explicit, counted slots):** `max_large_positions` (2, raised from 1 on 2026-09-23) and `max_pct_per_ticker_large` (25.0, raw collateral % of net liq per slot — two slots means up to 50% of net liq combined across both names, not a change to either one's own ceiling) — **cumulative** per-ticker collateral (what the account already holds in the name plus the new lot) above `max_collateral_per_ticker_pct` needs a free large-position slot and must still sit under this hard ceiling; without it, a high-priced name is either impossible or unbounded, and one number can't express a third option. Because it is cumulative, this pair doubles as the raw-collateral backstop wherever the risk-unit tallies can't be seeded from live IV (the order-approval re-gate, the single-ticker deep-dive, the CSP generator's sizer — none of which pass `iv_by_symbol`/`iv_of`): whatever those paths know about the candidate's own IV, one ticker can never exceed `max_pct_per_ticker_large` in raw collateral. `max_margin_usage_pct` (50.0) and `max_new_positions_per_run` (10) are unchanged. Both `strategies/cash_secured_put.py` (`screen_csp_candidates`) and `risk_engine.validate_candidates` call the same `capital.max_contracts` helper, so the generator and the gate can never disagree about how big a position may be (the root cause of D1). ### `src/ibkr/` — The IBKR connection layer

Handles everything that talks directly to Interactive Brokers via the `ib_async` library.

| File | What it does |
|---|
| `connection.py` | Opens and manages the connection to TWS/Gateway; enforces one connection per process. Provides a sync (`connect`) and async (`connect_async` / `async with`) connect with backoff, plus `AutoReconnect` — attached to the long-running daemons (approval service, monitor) it listens on `disconnectedEvent` and reconnects with capped backoff (re-subscribing market data on the monitor) so a TWS drop doesn't silently kill them. `connect_with_retry(ib, …, label=)` is a shared startup-connect helper: `connectAsync` wrapped in bounded exponential backoff (reuses `ibkr.reconnect.*`) so a fast stop→start cycle, where IB Gateway still holds the prior session's clientId (`Error 326` "client id is already in use"), self-heals instead of permanently disabling that connection (the 2026-06-24 03:42 case where `/account`/`/status` went dark). The approval service's exec + scan connections and the monitor connect through it. |
| `market_data.py` | Fetches live quotes, option chains, Greeks (delta/theta/IV), and historical IV data. Provides both a sync API and an `async` chain fetcher (`get_option_chain_quotes_async`) — the orchestrator uses the async one so every IBKR call runs on the `ib_async` event-loop thread (never a worker thread), respecting the line-limit batching. The strike band is **IV-scaled** (N6): `_strike_band_pct` returns `max(strike_band_pct, strike_band_iv_mult · IV · √(DTE/365))` from the symbol's latest stored IV (or a `universe.yaml → strike_bands` per-symbol override), so high-IV names widen enough to surface their ~0.25-delta strike instead of being clipped by a fixed ±15%. The auto-computed band is capped at `strike_band_max_pct` (S8) so an extreme-IV ETF can't generate a runaway strike/batch count; an explicit per-symbol override is exempt from the cap. **Qualification-storm guard:** after band-filtering, `_cap_strikes` keeps at most `max_strikes_per_symbol` strikes (nearest spot), and `_build_chain_contracts` then builds the cartesian **OTM-side-only per right** — calls only at strikes ≥ spot, puts only at strikes ≤ spot — instead of both rights across the whole band, since `covered_call.py`/`cash_secured_put.py` only ever keep OTM contracts (delta 0.20–0.35, both strategies alike as of 2026-09-23) and would discard the ITM half anyway; this roughly halves the qualify/quote batch count and wall-clock fetch time per symbol versus the old `strikes × expirations × 2` cartesian. `qualify_options_async` (in `contracts.py`) then qualifies that cartesian in `chain_batch_size` **chunks**, paced by `request_throttle_seconds`, each bounded by `qualify_timeout_seconds` — a single 768-contract `qualifyContractsAsync` burst of mostly-nonexistent weekly strikes is what flooded IBKR with `reqContractDetails`, tripped a pacing lockout, and (when the outer `symbol_timeout` then cancelled it mid-flight) wedged the whole ib_async session on 2026-06-22. **Line-leak guard:** every `reqMktData` line (option batches *and* spot snapshots) is opened/closed through `_open_line`/`_close_line`, which register the line in a module-level `_OPEN_LINES` table; `drain_market_data_lines(ib)` cancels every still-open line and is called by the orchestrator after any symbol times out or errors, so a fetch interrupted between opening and cancelling a line can't leak it against the ~100-line cap. **Half-dead-socket probe:** `probe_market_data_health(ib)` fires one fully-bounded snapshot quote on `health_probe_symbol` (qualify + tick-wait both timeout-capped, line always reclaimed) and returns a `ProbeHealth` record — `healthy=False` plus a `diagnosis`, an `action_hint`, and the IBKR error codes observed during the probe window (it hooks `ib.errorEvent` for the duration, since 1100/10197 arrive as events, not exceptions). The intraday loop calls it before each cycle because `ib.isConnected()` can't distinguish a live farm from a half-dead socket whose data requests silently never tick. **Diagnosis classification (2026-09-09):** the old probe collapsed every failure into "half-dead socket / Error 1100"; it now distinguishes Error 1100 (Gateway lost its upstream link — the original half-dead case), Error 10197 (competing live session: another login on the account holds the live-data entitlement, where a reconnect does nothing and the fix is closing the other session or using a second username), Errors 354/10089-10091 (no market-data subscription — an entitlement/config problem, not connectivity), 1101/1102 flaps (link being rebuilt — transient), and the codeless generic case (farm unreachable, nothing observed). The 🛑 *Scan blocked* Telegram message carries the diagnosis, the observed codes, and the matching action hint, so a 10197 block no longer tells the operator to "wait for the next cycle" as if a reconnect could fix it (the 2026-09-08 misdiagnosis). **Scan-efficiency (S3/S7):** the async path centres the strike band on `_resolve_spot_async`, which prefers `price_data.get_ohlcv`'s latest cached daily close (free after the first scan) and only falls back to a dedicated `reqMktData(snapshot=True)` when no cached close exists — skipping a ~2s spot snapshot per symbol per intraday cycle. Per-batch quote waits and the spot-snapshot wait are **event-driven** via `_await_ready`, with the old fixed `quote_sleep_seconds`/2s as a *ceiling*, not a floor. `_quote_ready(ticker, right=...)` waits for both a usable bid/ask **and** that side's open-interest tick (genericTick `101`) before the batch is considered ready — a well-behaved batch still returns early (~0.2–0.5s) once both arrive, otherwise it uses the full ceiling. (Fixed 2026-08-14: OI wasn't originally part of readiness, only bid/ask, so a batch would cancel its line before the OI tick — which streams in after bid/ask — had a chance to arrive; `passes_liquidity_gates` treats a missing OI as an automatic fail, so this was silently rejecting ~97% of quotes as illiquid universe-wide, even on names like AAPL/SPY.) Greeks are still not waited on (absent on delayed/paper data; the Black-Scholes fallback fills them), preserving most of the speedup on the target account. Cancel-between-batches line-cap discipline is unchanged. **Greeks fallback is layered IBKR-first (S2):** `_ticker_to_quote` takes the delta/IV from the first available IBKR per-contract computation (`modelGreeks` → `lastGreeks` → `askGreeks` → `bidGreeks` via `_pick_greeks`) so a momentarily-missing model tick still yields *genuine* IBKR greeks; quotes that have an IBKR IV but no delta are BS-filled locally by `_enrich_greeks_from_ibkr_iv` (no network) — Phase 1 extended this to fill **gamma/theta/vega** alongside delta (skipping only when *all four* are already present); only quotes IBKR could value *neither* greeks nor IV for fall through to the expensive `_enrich_greeks_yf` Yahoo download (now instrumented — it logs how many quotes forced a Yahoo fetch and how long it took). The `_enrich_greeks_yf` path likewise fills gamma/theta/vega from the BS formula after delta. The scan batch requests generic ticks `101,106` (option OI + implied vol) to capture the IBKR IV. Any BS-derived delta is labelled `greeks_source="black_scholes"` (never `"ibkr"`), so the F6 live-execution gate still rejects it as untrusted live greeks. |
| `portfolio.py` | Reads your current positions, account balance, buying power, and margin. `enrich_positions_with_greeks_async` populates option-position deltas from live model greeks (used by the EOD net-delta-exposure metric, which would otherwise read 0) — **qualifies contracts first** (fixed 2026-09-11, via `contracts.qualify_options_async`): `build_option`'s unqualified contract (`conId=0`) made ib_async's own `Contract.__hash__` raise inside `reqMktData`'s ticker bookkeeping before any network call, so every subscription silently failed and `.delta` was always `None`. |

### `src/data/` — The data abstraction layer

A thin provider abstraction between the analytics layer and the external market-data backends
(yfinance today; FMP/Polygon later). Analytics/strategies/engine never call ``yfinance.*``
directly — they go through the factories here, which read ``config/settings.yaml → data.*``
to pick the active backend and cache the instance process-wide. A future FMP/Polygon swap is a
config change, not a rewrite of every analytics module.

**IBKR is *not* a "provider" here** — it is the broker + execution path (``src/ibkr/``) and stays
untouched. This layer abstracts only the keyless external reads (prices, fundamentals, news
headlines) that the enrichment/deterministic analytics tiers share. The IBKR greeks fallback
in ``src/ibkr/market_data.py`` still calls ``yfinance`` directly and is out of scope by design.

| File | What it does |
|---|
| `protocols.py` | ``typing.Protocol`` classes (structural subtyping) every backend must implement: ``PriceProvider`` (``get_ohlcv``, ``get_last_price``), ``FundamentalsProvider`` (``get_info``, ``get_calendar``), ``NewsProvider`` (``get_headlines``), ``SymbolDirectoryProvider`` (``list_symbols`` — the full SEC filer directory), ``FilingsProvider`` (``get_company_facts(cik, *, etag=None) -> (dict, str | None)`` — the full XBRL companyfacts document for one filer, ``(payload, etag)``; empty payload on 404/missing-XBRL/304, never raises; on a 304 the input etag is returned so the caller can reuse its cached payload), and ``BulkPriceProvider`` (``get_daily_bars(symbol, lookback_days=400) -> pd.DataFrame`` — ascending daily OHLCV with a ``DatetimeIndex`` for the cold tier, where a keyed per-symbol API would be too expensive; empty frame when unavailable). ### `src/analytics/` — The analysis layer

HV30 (`_compute_hv30`) derives from the shared `price_data.get_ohlcv` settled-close history. | `realized_vol.py` | **Close-to-close annualized historical volatility** over a configurable window (C1). `compute_realized_vol(symbol, window=20)` reads the same `price_data.get_ohlcv` OHLCV store, computing log-return std × √252 × 100. | `market_conditions.py` | **The macro backdrop**, fetched once per scan (not per symbol) and returned as a `MarketConditions` schema attached to `ScanResult`. | `fair_value.py` | **Where a short option ought to be written, and for how much.** `compute_ideal_zone(...)` returns an `IdealZone`: (1) an **expected move** `spot · IV · √(DTE/365)`; (2) a **strike band** 0.30–1.20σ out for calls / 0.18–1.20σ out for puts (the inner edge is split per right since puts and calls don't map to the same sigma distance at equal delta — see the `risk_limits.yaml` row above), snapped to the symbol's own support/resistance (the first consumer of `TechnicalStats.support_levels`/`resistance_levels`, computed every scan and previously read by nothing), anchored to a 50d/200d SMA inside the band, widened for earnings-inside-DTE and a failed quality screen, nudged up for an ex-div inside a covered call's life, and floored at `cost_basis × min_strike_vs_basis`; (3) a **minimum credit** — Black-Scholes fair value at *realised* vol (HV30) plus `min_credit_edge_pct`, never below what the ROC/annualized-yield gates imply — making the variance-risk-premium thesis explicit. `compute_ideal_zone` prices this at the band's **anchor** strike (the right number for the abstract band); callers holding a specific contract must re-price it with **`zone_for_contract(zone, strike, iv, *, cost_basis)`**, which returns a copy whose `min_credit` is priced at the strike actually on offer and whose ROC/yield component divides by the denominator the strategy's gate really uses (cost basis for a CC, strike for a CSP). (D2) `min_credit` is now also read by `risk_engine.validate_candidates` and by both generators as the primary income gate (`income.require_vrp_edge`, reason `premium_below_fair_value`): the Rules Engine, not `fair_value.py`, is what rejects — this module has no reject path of its own, so the "Rules Engine is the sole path to an order" invariant is unchanged. Tunables live in `risk_limits.yaml → ideal_zone` / `income`. **Deterministic tier** — it may read technicals/IV/fundamentals/Black-Scholes and nothing else (enforced by `tests/test_eval_skills.py::test_fair_value_stays_in_the_deterministic_tier`). |

### `src/strategies/` — Trade candidate generators

Each module takes the analytics data and generates specific trades you could place.

| File | What it generates |
|---|
| `covered_call.py` | Covered call candidates: for each stock you own, finds the best call strike to sell (target delta, expiry, annualized yield). `screen_cc_candidates(...)` is the full screen returning a `ScreenResult` (passed **+ rejected + skip reason**); `generate_cc_candidates(...)` is the thin wrapper returning only the passing slate. `uncovered_call_capacity(positions, underlying) -> int` (fixed 2026-09-11) extracts `screen_cc_candidates`'s own sizing formula (`floor(long shares / 100) - existing short calls`) for a caller that only needs the number, not a full screen — `execution/approval.py`'s send-time share-ownership re-check uses it against a fresh position snapshot immediately before an order goes out. |
| `cash_secured_put.py` | Cash-secured put candidates: for `would_own` stocks, finds put strikes that offer good yield without excessive assignment risk. **Sized to headroom, not to the maximum affordable lot (D1):** `screen_csp_candidates(..., *, positions=None)` calls `engine.capital.resolve_caps`/`seed_budgets`/`max_contracts` — the same helper the Rules Engine uses — so contract count is the largest lot that fits every cash and concentration constraint at once (cash, per-ticker/sector risk units, the large-position slot), rather than the min of cash-and-CSP-budget alone; the two paths can no longer disagree about how big a position may be. `positions` seeds the running budgets from what the account already holds (both `scan.py` call sites pass the current snapshot; omitted it defaults to an empty portfolio). When no size fits at all, the candidate still renders at a display-only 1 lot tagged `insufficient_cash` (cash was the binding constraint) or `no_headroom` (a concentration/budget cap was) — the reason names which. Same `screen_csp_candidates` / `generate_csp_candidates` pairing as above. **Optional phase-aware reject (modernization Phase 3, default off):** when `risk_limits.yaml → cash_secured_put.reject_downtrend` is `true` and `tech_stats.phase == Phase.DOWNTREND`, the candidate is rejected with `REASON_PHASE_DOWNTREND` — ships disabled pending paper validation (see `STATUS.md`). `covered_call.py` gets the same `phase` field for display only; it never gates a CC, since the shares are already held. |
| `_evaluation.py` | Shared plumbing for screens that report **why** a contract was rejected. `ScreenResult` carries `passed`, `rejected` (`(candidate, reason_codes)`), `evaluated`, and `skipped` (set when the symbol never reached the per-quote loop at all — `not_in_would_own`, `no_long_shares`, `shares_already_covered`), plus a `tally()` for the diagnostic log. `rank_rejects(...)` orders rejects closest-to-passing: fewest distinct failed gates, then nearest the delta-band midpoint, then highest ROC. `display_premium(...)` prices a contract we only intend to *display* (falling back past `strict_mid` where the trading path never would — the accompanying `no_two_sided_market` reason marks it untrusted). Reason codes deliberately reuse the risk engine's vocabulary where the meaning matches, so one `_REJECT_REASON_LABELS` table humanizes both stages. **Every quote is evaluated against all gates rather than short-circuiting on the first failure**, so a reject carries its complete failure list and the ranking is meaningful. |
| `rolling.py` | Roll candidates: for existing short options approaching expiry or breaching delta limits, suggests the best roll-forward trade. Roll credit uses a **live quote** for the current contract; if no live quote is found the candidate is skipped. `generate_roll_candidates(..., *, defensive: bool = False)` runs **two separate economics (D4)**: the default **income** path rolls an unchallenged position forward and still requires a net credit clearing `income.min_roc_pct`/`min_annualized_yield_pct`; `defensive=True` (passed by every monitor-triggered caller, via `roll_pipeline.queue_roll_for_approval`) is for rescuing a challenged, deep-delta short — it skips the ROC/yield tests entirely and instead permits a bounded net debit (`monitor.roll_defensive.max_debit`), requires the new leg to cut `|delta|` by at least `min_delta_reduction`, and (when `require_breakeven_improvement` is set) requires the new strike to improve the breakeven whenever the roll isn't itself a credit. Requiring 1% ROC from a defensive roll made the candidate list empty exactly when the monitor's roll trigger fired, since a 0.60-delta short usually cannot be rolled to a safer delta for a credit. Both paths still populate `TradeCandidate.ideal` (Task 5's D2 follow-up), so the risk engine's variance-risk-premium gate reaches ROLL candidates regardless of which economics produced them. DTE is computed from `market_hours.today_et()`, not local `date.today()` — the earlier local-date read could fire `should_roll` a day early for an operator east of US/Eastern. |
| `buy_candidates.py` | Buy-to-own candidates: stocks from the watchlist worth buying specifically so you can sell covered calls against them. Scores each on IV rank, fundamental quality, and technical regime, then applies a score floor and count cap (`scoring_weights.yaml → buy_to_own.min_score` / `max_candidates`) so it surfaces only the strongest few — without the floor it returned every non-held, non-bearish universe name (e.g. 46/46 over a weekend). Also enriches each with price/IV/HV/VRP/RSI/SMA/earnings/dividend, an estimated monthly CC yield, a deterministic rationale, and the `sector` tag looked up from `universe.yaml → sectors` (used by `format_buy_list` to group candidates by sector). |
| `_scoring.py` | Shared scoring helpers used by all strategy modules. `technical_score` aligns regime to **short** premium (we sell): BULLISH favours selling puts (CSP), BEARISH/SIDEWAYS favours selling calls (CC) — the opposite of buy-side alignment. `fundamental_score` is a quality/dividend step function. `make_candidate_id` is a deterministic, **date-free** hash of `strategy\|underlying\|right\|strike\|expiry` (the property that lets the 15-min loop dedupe via `has_active_order`). `technical_score` also takes an optional `IdealZone` and blends in `fair_value.zone_fit_score` under the per-strategy `zone_fit` weight — the "support/resistance proximity" that `scoring_weights.yaml` has always described but never implemented. `zone_fit` **ships at 0.0**, so the default output is bit-identical to the regime-only score; enabling it is a deliberate human config change. |

### `src/engine/` — The decision and safety layer

This is where candidates get ranked and filtered.

| File | What it does |
|---|
| `capital.py` | **Capital arithmetic (D1, Task 9's `portfolio:` block consumes this).** Pure functions, no I/O, no config reads — callers pass the resolved `risk` dict. `risk_units(collateral, current_iv, dte)` computes capital-at-risk over the option's life (`collateral × IV × √(DTE/365)`; `current_iv` is a **percent**), returning `None` when IV is unavailable so callers fall back to a stricter raw-collateral cap rather than treating the position as risk-free. `resolve_caps(account, risk) -> Caps` resolves `risk_limits.yaml → portfolio` percentages into absolute dollar limits for one account snapshot, in a **fixed, non-configurable order**: the cash reserve (`cash_reserve_pct` / `cash_reserve_absolute`, greater-of) comes off `account.excess_liquidity` first, and the CSP budget (`max_csp_allocation_pct_of_deployable`) is a percentage of what remains — expressing both against gross cash would let the two knobs contradict each other. `seed_budgets(positions, sector_of, iv_of=None, *, caps=None) -> Budgets` seeds running tallies from current positions (a short put charges its assignment liability, matching how a new CSP candidate is charged). A position snapshot carries no IV of its own, so the third argument is a per-symbol IV lookup: supply it and **option** positions also charge the risk-unit tallies (`ticker_risk`/`sector_risk`) at their own DTE — without it those tallies start empty, and existing exposure counts for nothing against the per-ticker/per-sector concentration caps on the (common) path where the *candidate's* own IV is known. Callers that already hold portfolio-wide IV pass it (the full scan sweep via `risk_engine.validate_candidates(..., iv_by_symbol=...)`, `scripts/capacity_report.py`); callers where it would cost a fresh network round-trip on a latency-sensitive path (the order-approval re-validation gate, a single-ticker `/scan SYM` deep-dive) deliberately do not. Stock positions never get risk-unit seeding (no natural DTE) and neither does an expired or IV-less option — those, and every caller that omits `iv_of`, charge the raw-collateral tally only. The keyword-only `caps` (fixed 2026-09-11) seeds `Budgets.large_tickers` too: any symbol whose existing cumulative collateral already exceeds `max_ticker_collateral` starts the cycle already occupying a large-position slot, so a name already over cap purely from the book (no candidate has touched it yet) can't let `max_large_positions` further names go large on top of it; both production callers pass it, resolving `caps` immediately beforehand. `max_contracts(...) -> (contracts, binding)` finds the largest lot that fits every constraint at once (cash, CSP budget, ticker/sector risk units, and the large-position slot measured on **cumulative** per-ticker collateral — the marginal-lot comparison it replaces was blind to the book, so a name already at the ceiling looked empty to every new candidate). `strategies/cash_secured_put.py` sizes with it; `risk_engine.validate_candidates` gates with the same `resolve_caps`/`seed_budgets`/`charge` arithmetic plus an inline copy of `_fits`'s checks (it walks a ranked batch rather than growing one lot, so it can't call `_fits` directly) — the two must be kept in step, and that shared arithmetic is what makes the generator/gate disagreement that caused D1 unrepresentable. `charge(...)` mutates a `Budgets` in place so later candidates in the same batch see reduced headroom (the same greedy cumulative-consumption model the risk engine has always used, extended to risk units). `Budgets.large_tickers` is a **set of symbols**, not a count (fixed 2026-09-11): a slot belongs to a ticker, so `charge` adds a symbol to it whenever that ticker's CUMULATIVE collateral (not the marginal lot alone) crosses `max_ticker_collateral` — a second candidate on an already-large ticker is then a no-op add, never a second slot consumed, and `_fits`/`validate_candidates` only require a *new* free slot for a symbol not already in the set. |
| `scoring.py` | Normalizes raw analytics scores (IV rank, RSI, etc.) into a 0–100 scale |
| `decision_engine.py` | Combines scores with the configured weights, ranks candidates by total score, and selects the top N. `select_top_candidates_detailed(...)` returns `(top, dropped)` where each dropped candidate carries **why** — `"dedupe"` (a better strike on the same name won the slot) or `"top_n"` (`max_new_positions_per_run` was already full). Both used to vanish silently, making a full slate indistinguishable from a gated-out one; `select_top_candidates` remains as the thin wrapper. |
| `risk_engine.py` | **The safety gate.** Checks hard limits and returns PASS/REJECT with reasons. No AI. `validate_candidates` is **portfolio-aware**: it walks the ranked batch in priority order and enforces *cumulative* limits via `engine.capital` — deployable-cash feasibility, per-ticker/per-sector concentration in **risk units** (`max_risk_units_per_ticker_pct`/`max_risk_units_per_sector_pct`, falling back to raw-collateral `max_collateral_per_ticker_pct` when IV is unknown), the large-position slot (measured on **cumulative** per-ticker collateral, which is what makes it the raw-collateral backstop when `iv_by_symbol` is absent — see below), and total cash-secured-put collateral against the deployable-cash CSP budget — so multiple candidates can't each claim the whole account (D1, Task 9; see the `risk_limits.yaml` `portfolio:` block above for the full key reference). **Before any of that cumulative walk runs, `_select_budget_representatives` groups pass-1 survivors by `(underlying, strategy)` and keeps only the single highest-scoring one per group to spend the shared budget** — every other survivor in the group is rejected `dedupe_pre_gate` without ever touching the cumulative checks at all (2026-09-11 fix: a busy scan cycle's dozen-plus strikes/expiries on one name used to compete against each other for that name's own per-ticker budget, which is how 95 of 96 `concentration_limit` rejects over two weeks turned out to be a single symbol, TQQQ, held at zero); covered calls and rolls are excluded from grouping since they never reached the shared budget either way, and cross-symbol competition among the surviving representatives is unchanged. **A keyword-only `dedupe_same_symbol: bool = True` controls whether that grouping runs at all** (final-review fix): the default groups/dedupes exactly as above and is what every batch caller wants — the full scan sweep and the order-approval re-validation gate in `execution/approval.py` (which deliberately keeps it on so two approved-but-unexecuted orders on one underlying, approved in different scan cycles, can't both slip through); `False` skips `_select_budget_representatives` entirely, so every pass-1 survivor is its own representative and none is ever `dedupe_pre_gate`-rejected — the old single-pass greedy behaviour, restored on purpose for `scan._price_and_gate_ticker` (the `/scan TICKER` deep-dive, and the `promote` handler that reuses its pricing path), a browse/compare view whose whole point is showing every strike on one ticker that individually qualifies, not picking a winner among them. The optional `iv_by_symbol=` argument is forwarded to `capital.seed_budgets` so the account's *existing* option positions charge those risk-unit budgets too, instead of the batch's candidates only being measured against each other; the full scan sweep passes the IV map it already computed, and the two paths that would have to buy that data with a new network call (the order-approval re-validation gate, the single-ticker deep-dive) omit it by design — on those, existing exposure reaches the caps through the **cumulative** per-ticker collateral comparison in the large-position check, which bounds any one ticker at `max_pct_per_ticker_large` regardless of what is known about the candidate's own IV. It also gates per candidate: the variance-risk-premium floor (D2, primary — `income.require_vrp_edge`, reject when `premium < candidate.ideal.min_credit`, reason `premium_below_fair_value`; a missing ideal zone never blocks), ROC/yield noise floors (`income.min_roc_pct`/`min_annualized_yield_pct`, now secondary — see `fair_value.py` below for why), IV-rank floor (when known), DTE window, delta (required for income strategies), and the earnings blackout. **Those three income gates (ROC, annualized yield, VRP) apply to the income strategies only.** A `Strategy.ROLL` is a repair, not an income trade: a defensive roll books `roc_pct = 0.0` by construction and pays a premium deliberately under the new strike's fair value, so leaving rolls inside the gates rejected *every* defensive roll — structurally, at the approval-queue re-gate in `execution/approval.py`, after the operator had already approved the card. A roll's economics are bounded by `strategies/rolling.py`'s own `monitor.roll_defensive` knobs (`max_debit`, `min_delta_reduction`, `require_breakeven_improvement`); its `ideal` zone stays populated and visible on the card as display/audit. `validate_live_quote` is the second pass at execution time, re-checking a single candidate against the fresh live quote (delta drift / collapsed mid). |

Handles everything between your Telegram approval and the order reaching IBKR.

### `src/backtest/` — Offline income backtest

A standalone, deterministic simulator for the CC/CSP income strategies. It is **fully isolated
from the live path** — it imports nothing from `engine/` or `execution/` and is never imported by
them — so it can never influence a real order. **v1** synthesises premiums with Black-Scholes from
trailing realised vol (a fair-value IV proxy → expected edge ≈ 0 by construction). **v2 (N21)**
prices the entry premium from the symbol's *stored* daily IV (`iv_history`), so the result actually
measures the variance-risk premium (IV−HV); it also simulates the 50% profit-take and IV-rank
gating. Use it to compare parameter choices, not as tick-accurate truth.

| File | What it does |
|---|
| `engine.py` | The pure core. `simulate(symbol, prices, params, *, iv_series=None)` walks a daily-close series, writes non-overlapping short-premium cycles, settles each cash-style at expiry, and returns a `BacktestResult`. With `iv_series` it prices entries at the stored IV (measuring VRP) and reports `mean_vrp_pct` + `iv_source`; `params.profit_take_pct` closes a cycle early when its daily mark decays to the take level (`profit_take_rate`); `params.min_iv_rank` only opens cycles whose backtest IV rank clears the bar. Takes in-memory series → unit-testable offline. |
| `data.py` | yfinance loaders: `load_price_series` (daily closes), `load_iv_series` (forward-filled `iv_history` IV aligned to the price dates, the v2 entry-IV source), and **C10** `load_earnings_dates` (returns sorted historical earnings dates from `ticker.earnings_dates`; empty list for ETFs / network failures, never raises). Kept out of `engine.py` so the core needs no network/DB. |
| `report.py` | Plain-text renderers for the CLI. `format_report` — full `BacktestResult` summary (pricing-IV source, mean VRP, profit-take rate). **C11** `compact_report` — 4-line summary of a `BacktestResult` suitable for injecting into Claude prompts or displaying at decision time. **C10** `format_earnings_cycle_report` — human-readable summary of an `EarningsCycleBacktestResult`. |
| `earnings.py` | **C10** Earnings-cycle backtest + vol-crush mode. `simulate_earnings_cycles(symbol, prices, params, earnings_dates, *, blackout_before, blackout_after, vol_crush_dte, iv_series)` segments the price series by historical earnings dates and evaluates strategy performance across each inter-earnings window, gating entries when the window is too narrow for the DTE. The optional vol-crush mode also enters a short-DTE trade right after each earnings release while IV is still elevated. Returns an `EarningsCycleBacktestResult` with per-cycle detail (pre-earnings trade, vol-crush trade, gate flag) and aggregate win-rate / P&L. Pure and offline: takes in-memory series, never touches the DB or network. |
| `on_demand.py` | **C11** Reusable, candidate-aware core behind the CLI **and** the strategist-prompt injection. `params_from_candidate(cand, *, profit_take_pct, min_iv_rank)` maps a live `TradeCandidate`'s delta/DTE/contracts into `BacktestParams`; `run_backtest(symbol, params, *, …, earnings, …)` loads history and runs the right simulation, returning a `BacktestOutcome` (standard / earnings result / human error message — never raises); `summarize(outcome)` and `backtest_candidate(cand)` return a compact text block. **Fence:** enrichment for verdict/ranking only — reaches Claude solely through the prompt builder, never imported by `engine/`, `execution/`, or the sizing path. |

Driven by `scripts/backtest.py` (`--use-stored-iv`, `--profit-take`, `--min-iv-rank` enable v2) and **C11** `scripts/backtest_candidate.py` (on-demand per-candidate backtest, supports `--earnings` mode and `--compact` output for decision-time review) — both now thin wrappers over `on_demand.run_backtest`. The same core is the **Claude-invocable action**: `strategist.build_prompt` injects a per-candidate `backtest_candidate(cand)` line when `claude.backtest_in_prompt` is enabled (default OFF — it adds a yfinance fetch per candidate at prompt-build time). The Black-Scholes price (`analytics.black_scholes.bs_price`) was added for this harness; `analytics.black_scholes.bs_delta` is reused to delta-target strikes.

### `src/orchestrator/` — Daily workflows

Ties everything together into the daily automated routines.

The scan is deliberately split three ways, so that *producing* a scan's data and *presenting* it
are separable concerns: `scan_pipeline.py` produces per-symbol data, `scan_progress.py` owns
everything a human sees (both the live progress messages and the end-of-run sends), and `scan.py`
sequences the two, aggregates the run-level state, gates and persists. The load-bearing rule is
that **`scan_pipeline.py` imports nothing from `src/notify/`** — enforced by
`tests/test_eval_skills.py::test_scan_pipeline_does_not_import_the_notify_layer` — so any caller
that only wants a `ScanResult` (the 15-min daemon loop, a future HTTP API) can trigger a scan
without acquiring a Telegram dependency it could never shed.

`scan_progress.py` is intended to be the only module of the trio importing `src/notify/`, and it
holds every Telegram call *except three*: `scan.py`'s module import block still names
`send_candidates`, `send_buy_list` and `send_account_snapshot`, which it injects into
`send_scan_results` as a `SendDeps` bundle. That is a **test constraint, not a design choice** —
`tests/test_scan_timeout.py`, `tests/test_scan_materiality.py` and `tests/test_scan_review_reuse.py`
monkeypatch those three as attributes of `src.orchestrator.scan`, so moving the references would
make the stubs silently miss and let the suite send real Telegram traffic. Same reason
`scan_pipeline.SymbolDeps` injects its screens. (`run_ticker_scan` also imports
`format_ticker_scan_result` function-locally; that path is untouched by the split.)

| File | What it does |
|---|
| `eod_report.py` | Spawned by `scripts.start`'s built-in scheduler at 4:15 PM ET (configurable via `scheduler.eod_report`; no cron job needed). Pulls the day's **option premium cashflow** (SELL credits − BUY debits — surfaced as "premium cashflow", not a paired realized P&L; assignment stock-leg P&L is excluded, N13), generates a journal entry via Claude, sends an end-of-day summary to the **account snapshot thread** (thread 58). Also appends today's ATM IV observation per universe symbol (N4, `_append_daily_iv`) so the IV-rank window stays current, and reconciles the verdict ledger + saves the position snapshot. **Phase 6**: for each detected assignment (`assigned_shorts`) it also calls `mark_campaign_assigned(symbol, assignment_price=strike, right=…)` so the wheel campaign's adjusted cost basis is maintained (C6) — previously `mark_campaign_assigned` was unreachable in production. **The whole run is idempotent**: `_write_journal` (step 6) upserts the day's `JournalRow` — query by `entry_date`, replace every field but `created_at` — mirroring `storage/positions.py::save_position_snapshot`'s pattern instead of the bare `session.add()` it used to be. `JournalRow.entry_date` carries a unique constraint, so the old insert-only write raised `IntegrityError` on a second same-day run and aborted before step 6b ever executed, silently skipping the reconciler, assignment auto-detection, and tomorrow's position-snapshot baseline for the rest of that day. A repeat now replaces the journal row with the re-run's numbers (a re-run exists to produce better numbers, not to be ignored) and reaches step 6b every time. |

Claude-memory injection is bounded (N9): `_load_memory` keeps at most `_MEMORY_ROWS_PER_SYMBOL` (3) rows per symbol, outcomes first; `_persist_memory` upserts one `claude_memory` row per (symbol, strategy, day) instead of inserting one per surfaced candidate per 15-min cycle. Interactive `/scan` progress is driven by `_Tracker` (defined in `scan_progress.py` and imported back here). The 15-min daemon loop runs the scan with both callbacks `None` (silent) and `intraday=True`, which enables the **S1 materiality gate**: `_compute_material_symbols` restricts the per-symbol option-chain fetch to held positions that *rose* past `held_position_move_pct` (2%, directional — up only, since that's what opens room for a new OTM CC strike; a drop doesn't, and existing-position risk is handled continuously by the separate event-driven monitor, not this gate — previously this was unconditional every cycle regardless of direction, the single biggest fixed IBKR chain-fetch cost in the loop), `actively_wheeling` names (`universe.yaml`) whose live spot moved past `intraday_rescan_move_pct` (0.5%), `would_own`-but-not-`actively_wheeling` ("dip-watch") names whose live spot *dropped* past `dip_pull_in_pct` (3%, directional — a rally never counts), names that cleared the score floor last cycle, and any `actively_wheeling`/held name whose *own* last fetch exceeds `force_full_scan_minutes` — checked **per symbol** (2026-08-27), not "sweep the whole core together the instant the single stalest one crosses the line," so refreshes desynchronize and spread out over time instead of bursting all 19 at once; dip-watch names are never swept on this timer, only event-triggered by a genuine drop. **The bucket rules are UNIONed, not selected between (2026-08-28):** a symbol in more than one bucket is tested against every rule that applies to it and any one firing is enough, so holding shares can never *reduce* a name's coverage — it only adds the rally trigger. (The old `if held / elif dip_watch / else` chain let `held` win outright, which silently demoted every held `actively_wheeling` name from the 0.5% either-way gate to 2%-up-only, shrinking the core rotation to just the names the operator didn't hold; the CSP screen runs for every `would_own` name regardless of holding, so a held wheel name has a live CSP reason to refetch on a dip that the CC-oriented held rule cannot see.) Each fetched symbol also records **its own** fetch timestamp rather than one shared run-level stamp, so a long sweep's cohort goes stale spread over the span the sweep took instead of all inside one later cycle — a burst longer than `intraday_loop_minutes` overruns the cycle and costs the next one. A per-cycle ceiling, `market_data.chain_fetch_budget_seconds` (600s, 2026-08-28), caps how long one cycle may spend fetching chains: the gate's filtering collapses when correlation goes to 1 (a broad sell-off makes every `would_own` name material at once, ~23 min of fetching inside a 15-min cycle), and an overrun sets `scan_running`, skipping the NEXT cycle's scan outright. Over-budget symbols are demoted to immaterial (analytics still run) and carried in `ScanResult.unreached_symbols` to the next cycle's `must_include_symbols`, where `_fetch_priority` ranks them first — so a cluster drains over consecutive on-time cycles. Spending order is retry > cleared-floor > movers by multiple of their own bar (`_move_ratio`, the same function the gate uses as its boolean, so the two cannot drift) > staleness-only. Sized from a measured fit, `duration = 56s + 29.9s x fetches`: 900 - 56 - 150 (`symbol_timeout_seconds` of un-preemptable overshoot) = 694, rounded down to 600. Never applies to a manual /scan. Immaterial names are skipped this cycle (analytics still run for every name in `would_own` ∪ holdings, so the buy-to-own list stays complete). Each fetched symbol's `scan_state` baseline is upserted after scoring (S10). In `intraday` mode it also enables the **S5 LLM-skip**: `_candidates_review_hash` hashes the top candidates' `candidate_id` + signal vectors, and when that matches the prior cycle's hash (stored in the `last_review_hash` system setting) the `claude -p` review is skipped and the persisted `ClaudeReview`s are reused via `_load_prior_reviews` — enrichment-only, the risk gate already ran, so this never affects gating (the fence). Manual `/scan` leaves `intraday=False` and always sweeps the full universe (dip_watch names seed-only unless gapped ≥3% overnight — see below), reviews fresh, and sends in full. After the risk gate, `run_scan` also computes per-strategy **near-misses** — the top `_NEAR_MISS_LIMIT` (1) highest-scoring contract that did *not* make `passed` (failed the gate, or cleared it but fell below the score floor), plus a count of any further rejects — and passes them (`near_misses`/`near_miss_more`) to `send_candidates`, so an empty CC/CSP screen on any cycle (including the 15-min loop) names the single closest trade, why it was rejected, and "…and N more" when the rest were truncated. **Single-ticker scan:** `run_ticker_scan(ib, ticker, *, bot, chat_id, progress_msg_id)` is a slimmer on-demand variant for the `/scan AAPL` command: validates the ticker via `qualifyContractsAsync` (raises `TickerNotFoundError` if unknown), fetches the option chain, runs analytics and CC/CSP/buy-candidate generation (CC only if the ticker is held), applies scoring and the risk gate, and edits the Telegram progress message in place with `format_ticker_scan_result`. For any strategy that turned up no qualifying option it identifies the best-scoring *failed* contract (the near miss) and its specific gate reasons (from `scored` — sorted desc — and the verdict map), so the card shows the closest strike and exactly what it failed on. When a strategy has *neither* a qualifying candidate *nor* a near-miss — the symbol never reached a single quote, e.g. `LABU` sitting outside `would_own` for a CSP or shares not held for a CC — it separately computes `cc_hypothetical`/`csp_hypothetical`, an `IdealZone` from `fair_value.compute_ideal_zone` priced directly off `tech_stats`/`iv_stats`/`fund_stats` (no option chain needed) at the strategy's configured mid-DTE, purely so the card still has a fair-value number to show; it also threads the CSP screen's `skipped` reason through as `csp_skip_reason` (e.g. `not_in_would_own`) so the card can say *why* rather than just going quiet. Neither reaches the risk gate, the score, or the allowlist — informational only. It also derives a `greeks_fallback` flag from the chain quotes for the honest sources footer. And — like the full scan, enrichment-only — it runs the best CC + best CSP through `review_candidates` (the configured Claude/Ollama backend) off-thread for a `🤖` verdict; a review failure is non-fatal and the card simply renders without it. The single-ticker call is a **holistic deep-dive**: the macro/sector backdrop is **always** gathered (off-thread, in parallel: this ticker's prior-recommendation memory `_load_memory([ticker])`, the macro `MarketConditions` via `get_market_conditions`, and the `SectorContext` via `get_sector_context`) regardless of whether any contract qualifies — so the deterministic "🌐 Market & Sector" line never depends on a tradeable trade existing. The LLM "🧠 Read" reviews the best CC + best CSP; **when neither cleared the gate it falls back to reviewing the closest near-miss per strategy** so the deep-dive still produces a synthesis instead of going silent (these near-misses surface only as the overall `summary`, never as a per-candidate verdict — the card shows verdicts only beside a qualifying contract; the `/scan TICKER` path never executes, so the fence is untouched). `review_candidates` is called with `sector_context=...`, `single_ticker=True`, and `analytics=analytics_map` (the per-symbol raw signals). `single_ticker=True` switches the prompt to a gate-neutral framing and additionally requests a plain-English `summary` that *explains what each metric means* and synthesizes sentiment from the VIX regime + sector backdrop. The card renders the deterministic "🌐 Market & Sector" line (VIX regime + sector/SPY/ticker returns + relative strength) and the "🧠 Read" block (the LLM `summary`, shown once). A full sweep also tallies `ScanResult.provenance` (a `ProvenanceCounts`) — per-symbol option-chain success/failure/skip, which source produced each symbol's spot price (IBKR chain parity vs. yfinance), VIX availability, and the IBKR-vs-Black-Scholes split of option Greeks — and sends it as a final `format_data_provenance` Telegram message once the scan completes, so an operator can see at a glance which data sources were live this run. **Dip_watch seed-only at startup / manual /scan (2026-08-28):** the full-sweep path (the first cycle since process start, and manual `/scan`) no longer unconditionally chain-fetches dip_watch names — each gets a yfinance probe, and only names that gapped ≥3% overnight (bidirectional — a gap-up is a legitimate CSP setup at the open via IV expansion / news; a gap-down is the dip rule) get a chain fetch. Quiet names and names with no persisted baseline (first-ever run) are seed-only: the probe price is persisted as `last_spot` with `last_scanned_at=NULL` (so the per-cycle rule (c) drop gate works from cycle 1 against a yfinance-sourced baseline, with no IBKR/yfinance mismatch), at the cost of forgoing the first-cycle IV/Greeks set for quiet dip_watch names — acceptable, since a dip_watch name that hasn't gapped overnight isn't a candidate anyway. `/scan TICKER` still force-fetches a single named ticker on demand. **(P2-M4 Task 4.2) `_price_and_gate_ticker(ib, ticker)`** is `run_ticker_scan`'s chain-fetch-through-risk-gate steps (chain fetch, analytics, positions/account fetch, CC/CSP screens, scoring, the `validate_candidates` gate, and the `record_assessments` audit-trail write) extracted into their own async helper returning a `TickerPricingResult` (`quotes`, a new `chain_error` field nothing previously surfaced, `scored`, `verdict_map`, `min_score`, `cc_passed`/`csp_passed`, `ticker_assessed`, plus the analytics/portfolio data `run_ticker_scan` needs downstream). `run_ticker_scan` calls it and continues unchanged with near-miss detection, hypothetical zones, the buy candidate, the Claude/Ollama review, and the Telegram card — a pure refactor, zero behavior change. `TickerPricingAborted` (carrying a `"analytics"`/`"account"` stage) replaces the old inline early-return-with-Telegram-edit for those two failure points, so `run_ticker_scan` still edits the same error message, just from a caught exception instead of an inline `return`. | `scan_pipeline.py` | The per-symbol half of a scan, and **data only** — no Telegram, no progress tracker, no run-level state. `scan_symbol(ib, symbol, *, material, account, positions, would_own, probed_spot, symbol_timeout_seconds, deps)` runs one symbol's option-chain fetch (bounded by `symbol_timeout_seconds`, draining leaked market-data lines on timeout/error), spot resolution (`infer_spot_from_quotes`), analytics + sentiment concurrently off-thread, and the CC/CSP screens with `apply_sentiment` stamped onto every contract they produced (passed *and* rejected — both are rendered on the same cards). Every stage of the pipeline writes its results here. This means:
- If a scan crashes halfway through, the next run can pick up where it left off.
- The dashboard reads from this database — no IBKR connection needed.
- You can inspect any trade decision by querying the database.

### `src/api/` — The FastAPI web layer

A separate process (`scripts/run_api.py`, port 8787 by default) that holds **no `ib_async`
connection and no clientId** — it cannot reach the broker, by construction
(`tests/test_web_fence.py::test_the_api_never_constructs_a_broker_connection` greps for
`ib_async` under `src/api/` and `src/research/` and fails the build if it finds any). The
import direction is one-way: the web layer imports from the trading system (`src.storage`,
`src.common.config`), but nothing under `engine/`, `execution/`, or `strategies/` may import
`src.api` or `src.research` — `test_web_fence.py::test_trading_path_never_imports_the_web_layer`
enforces it by grepping those three packages' source text.

### `src/research/` — The research data layer (web tier)

A **second** SQLite database at `data/research.db`, separate from `data/income_system.db` on
purpose. SQLite permits one writer per database; a nightly research ingest writing into the
trading database would serialise against `approval_service`'s writes, and the failure mode is
a delayed trade approval. Two databases means two writers never contend. The research
`Base` (in `src/research/store/models.py`) is a distinct `DeclarativeBase` from
`src/storage/models.Base` — `create_all()` can never build the wrong schema against the wrong
engine, and the import is one-way: **the trading system never imports `src.research`** (the
`tests/test_web_fence.py` fence test enforces this — see §4.7 of the web design).

Ingestion runs as its own process (`scripts/run_research_worker.py`) — an APScheduler
`BackgroundScheduler`, the same as `src/api/`, it holds **no `ib_async` connection and no
clientId**. It writes only to `data/research.db`; the fence test above also covers it. A job
that raises is logged and swallowed (`ingest/jobs.py::run_job`) so one failing source can
never take the scheduler, or every other registered job, down with it — a heartbeat
(`worker_heartbeat` table) is written only after a job **succeeds**, so `/health`'s
`worker_heartbeat` field cannot lie about an outage.

A `remove` for a symbol in `actively_wheeling` is ignored when composing `would_own` specifically (you can't stop being willing to own something you're actively wheeling). Reads go through `src.storage.db.session_scope()`, not the API's write-scoped `trading_session`/`get_command_engine` fence — this module only reads. Results are cached in-process for `_TTL_SECONDS` (60s, a plain `(dict, time.monotonic())` module-level tuple — no caching library); `invalidate_universe_cache()` is the escape hatch a command drain calls so its own process sees an edit immediately rather than up to 60s later. **Fail-safe:** any exception reading the overrides table (locked SQLite, missing table, anything) is caught broadly, logs a warning, and returns the YAML base untouched — never raises, never empties the universe. Separate from `get_config()` deliberately: `get_config()` is `functools.lru_cache`'d with no invalidation and is called from the risk engine, so composing inside it would both import a storage module into `config.py` and never become visible without a process restart. |

**Rule:** Modules never pass raw IBKR objects to each other — they always convert to these shared
schemas first. This keeps modules independent and testable.

## Process architecture (what runs where)

The system splits into separate processes, each with its own IBKR client ID, to avoid conflicts.
The full registry lives in `config/settings.yaml → ibkr.client_ids`; **never reuse an id across two
processes that run at the same time.**

Applying a command — mutating `ApprovalRow`, inserting `OrderRow`, and marking the `AppCommandRow` `applied` — is **one transaction** in the exec process, so a crash mid-apply never leaves the intent half-done. `dedupe_key` (`f"{kind}:{target}"` for kinds whose handler is not itself idempotent, `NULL` for kinds — including universe_add/universe_remove — whose handler is an idempotent upsert and may repeat harmlessly) makes two clicks on Approve produce one command, not two. `confirm_token` is set for live-mode order-reaching intents awaiting a second human confirm (§4.6). | `ClaudeReview` / `RollReview` | Claude's structured review of a candidate / a live position roll. `ClaudeReview.summary` is a plain-English synthesis populated **only on the single-ticker `/scan TICKER` path** (the prompt requests it via `single_ticker=True`): it explains what the metrics mean and reads the VIX/sector backdrop into an overall sentiment; empty on the full-universe path. |

## Key invariants (things that must never change)

1. **The Rules Engine is the only path to execution.** Nothing bypasses it.
2. **Claude never places, sizes, or blocks orders.** It enriches; it does not control.
3. **One IBKR client ID per process.** Sharing IDs causes connection conflicts.
4. **Secrets live only in `.env`.** Never in code, logs, or YAML.
5. **All option orders use LimitOrder at mid-price.** Never market orders.
6. **`qualifyContracts` runs before every order submission.**
7. **The fence: the enrichment learning loop never reaches the engine.** `src/claude/eval/` (the
   outcome ledger, close reconciler, and score-vs-outcome analysis) is read-only with respect to
   trading — it observes and measures, never gates, sizes, or ranks. The risk engine, scoring, and
   sizing never import `src/claude/eval/` — gates/weights/limits stay human-edited config. Enforced
   by `tests/test_eval_skills.py::test_skills_never_reach_the_engine`.

## Operational risks & how they're handled

The hard parts of this system are operational, not algorithmic. The main failure modes and their
mitigations:

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|
| **IB Gateway disconnects mid-session** | `AutoReconnect` on the daemons (backoff + re-subscribe); the monitor self-heals on the next poll; one-shots simply abort and retry on the next scheduled run. |
| **Market-data line limit (~100)** | Option chains are requested in batches and cancelled between batches; only one symbol's chain is fetched at a time. |
| **clientId conflicts** | Central registry in `settings.yaml`; one id per concurrent process (see the process table above). On a fast restart, where Gateway still holds the prior session's id (`Error 326`), the daemons connect via `connect_with_retry` (backoff until the id frees) and the launcher waits `STARTUP_GRACE_SECONDS` first. |
| **Claude unavailable / unparseable** | Strict JSON validation + graceful fallback — the Rules-Engine-approved list still ships to Telegram. Claude never blocks the pipeline. |
| **Approval→execution timing gap** | Orders queue in the DB; the executor re-validates against a fresh live quote + the Rules Engine at send time; off-hours orders wait for RTH; stale approvals expire (TTL). |
| **Order rejects / partial fills / wrong contract** | `qualifyContracts` before every order; LimitOrder at mid (never market); fill monitoring with timeout/cancel; rejects alert back to Telegram. |
| **Early assignment (dividends/ITM)** | The monitor flags ITM-ish short calls near ex-dividend and short options breaching delta/DTE, prompting a roll alert. |
| **Risk-limit bypass / runaway** | The deterministic Rules Engine runs twice (decision time + send time); a buying-power buffer is always reserved; live trading is triple-gated. |
| **Secrets leakage** | `.env` is gitignored and never logged; a Telegram chat-id allowlist means only you can approve. |
| **Paper↔live confusion** | Live execution requires `LIVE_TRADING=true` **and** the live port; a loud startup banner states the active mode and account; each live order needs a second `[CONFIRM LIVE]` tap. |

Database concurrency (multiple processes on one SQLite file) is handled with WAL mode + a 30 s busy
timeout, and no process holds a write transaction open across network I/O.
